## Title

PHOSPHATE OVERACCUMULATOR 2 (PHO2) is a negative regulator of arbuscular mycorrhizal symbiosis

## Abstract

Arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) symbiosis is a mutualistic association between approximately 80% of land plants and fungi from the phylum Glomeromycotina. AM fungi enhance plant access to essential nutrients and water beyond the rhizosphere in exchange for photosynthetically derived carbon. This mutualistic symbiosis presents a promising tool for sustainable agriculture, as it not only supports plant nutrition but also immunity and soil health. However, AM symbiosis is suppressed by increased phosphate (Pi) availability, and our understanding of the underlying molecular regulation is incomplete. Here, we identify Phosphate overaccumulator 2 (PHO2) as a key negative regulator of AM colonisation under Pi-sufficient conditions. PHO2 encodes an E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme that regulates plant Pi homeostasis by promoting the turnover of proteins involved in Pi uptake and translocation. The pho2 mutants in both rice (Oryza sativa) and Nicotiana benthamiana retained high AM fungal colonisation even in high Pi conditions, whereas overexpression of PHO2 under Pi-depletion reduced AM engagement. Transcriptomic analyses revealed that PHO2 activity regulates expression of genes required for epidermal fungal entry, including the strigolactone biosynthesis pathway, fungal perception, and Common Symbiosis Signalling Pathway (CSSP). Together, our findings present a new role for PHO2 as a negative regulator of early establishment of AM colonisation.

This ENA study contains 32 paired-end mRNA-seq libraries of Oryza sativa Japonica Group cv. Nipponbare roots (wild type and pho2) grown at low (25 uM) or high (250 uM) phosphate, with mock inoculation or Rhizophagus irregularis, four biological replicates per condition. Sequenced 150 bp paired-end on Illumina NovaSeq 6000 (Novogene, Cambridge, UK).
