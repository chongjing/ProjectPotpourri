Materials and Methods

Generation of a phylogenetic tree

PHO2 sequences from Arabidopsis thaliana (At2g33770) and rice (LOC_Os05g48390) were used as queries to identify homologous sequences using the Python script, collect_best_BLAST_hits.py v0.3 60. Homologs were retrieved from seven additional plant species: Medicago truncatula, Lotus japonicus, Nicotiana benthamiana, Solanum lycopersicum, Hordeum vulgare, Triticum aestivum, and Zea

mays. All collected homolog sequences were aligned with the protein alignment program, MAFFT v7.505 61. Alignment columns with an occupancy below 10 % were removed with the Python script, algntrim.py v0.15 60. The alignment was subjected to the phylogenetic tool, IQ-TREE v1.6.12 62 with 100 rounds of classic (slow) bootstrapping to infer a phylogeny using the model finder. The best-fit model used for the final tree construction was JTT+F+R5. Finally, the online phylogenetic tree viewer, iTOL v6 63, was used to select and visualise the orthologous clade containing Arabidopsis and rice PHO2.

Rice materials

All rice lines used in this study belong to the Oryza sativa L. ssp. Japonica cultivar, Nipponbare. The Tos17-retrotransposon insertion lines, pho2-1 (NF2586) and pho2-2 (NE9017), were provided by the Rice Genome Resource Centre, Japan 64. PHO2 overexpression lines were generated in the pho2-1 mutant background using Golden Gate cloning, described below. All lines were confirmed as homozygous using PCR-based genotyping primers listed in Table S2.

AM colonisation assay of rice

Seeds were briefly washed in 70 % (v/v with water) ethanol, before rinsing with sterile deionised water. Seeds were then sterilised on a shaker in 3 % sodium hypochlorite (v/v with water) at room temperature for 20 minutes. In a laminar flow hood, seeds were washed three times in sterile MilliQ water to remove the sodium hypochlorite solution. Seeds were then transferred to 0.6% bacto-agar (w/v with water) plates and allowed to germinate for 3-6 days at 30 °C in the dark.

Unless stated otherwise, seedlings were transferred to a 0.5L volume pot containing autoclaved silica 14/25 sand. Seedlings received a mock inoculation of RO water or an inoculation of 600 Rhizophagus irregularis spores. Spores were freshly extracted from hairy carrot (Daucus carota L.) root organ cultures and resuspended in water. For a cone (2.5 cm internal diameter, 12 cm depth) experiment, 300 AM spores were inoculated in the same sand substrate. Rice plants were grown in a walk-in controlled environment growth chamber (CONVIRON EVO series) with 12 hr light/12 hr

dark cycle and 350 µEm-2 sec-1 light intensity at 28/20 °C (light/dark), with 65 % relative humidity.

For the first week following their transfer to sand, seedlings were watered three times weekly with reverse osmosis (RO)-purified water. For subsequent weeks, plants were watered once and fertilised twice weekly with half-strength Hoagland’s nutrient solution containing low (25 µM; LP) or high (250 µM; HP) phosphate unless stated otherwise (Table S1). Hoagland’s solution was administered with 0.01 % (w/v) iron supplement, Sequestrene Rapid (Syngenta).

At the point of harvest, roots were washed with RO water to remove sand and then gently patted dry with paper towel. Photos were taken and the fresh weight of roots and shoots was measured. Samples were taken for fungal quantification, molecular analyses and phosphate measurements.

AM fungal structure quantification

Roots were cut into approximately 2 cm pieces and mixed well to collect representative root samples for staining. Initially, roots were incubated in 10 % KOH (w/v in water), stained with 0.1 % Trypan blue solution comprised of 0.1 % (w/v) Trypan blue, 50 % (v/v) lactic acid, and 50 % (v/v) glycerol and mounted on a glass slide. Roots were observed using a DM750 Binocular microscope (Leica Microsystems GmbH, Germany) at 20x magnification and percentage root length colonisation was quantified following a modified gridline intersection method 65,66 counting presence of intraradical hyphae, hyphopodia, arbuscules and vesicles. Total root length colonisation was calculated as percentage of root length containing any AM colonisation structure inside the roots using the AMScorer spreadsheet 67.

To capture detailed arbuscule images, a representative mix of root pieces was stored in 50 % ethanol (v/v with water) at room temperature before staining with Wheat Germ Agglutinin conjugated to Alexa Fluor™ 488 (WGA-Alexafluor 488; Invitrogen™, USA) diluted to 0.2 mg/mL in phosphate-buffered saline solution (PBS; 137 mM NaCl, 3 mM KCl, 10 mM Na2HPO4, 2 mM KH2PO4, pH 7.4). Stained samples were stored in the dark at 4 °C for at least 6 hours. Immediately prior to imaging, roots were counterstained in propidium iodide solution (5 mg/mL in PBS) for 1-2 minutes to visualise plant cell walls. Root

fragments were mounted on a glass slide in PBS. Samples were imaged using a Stellaris 8 Falcon confocal microscope (Leica Microsystems GmbH, Germany) with excitation by a white light laser at 5 % power at 488 nm. Emission was detected by hybrid detectors at 500-550 nm and 600-700 nm to detect WGA-Alexafluor 488 and propidium iodide emission, respectively. Representative images of arbuscules were obtained using a 40x water immersion objective. To determine arbuscule size, images were captured with a 20x dry objective. All image processing and analysis was performed using Fiji/ImageJ 68,69. Arbuscule size was determined by dividing arbuscule area by the area of the surrounding cell. All visible arbuscules (100-200) were measured from 3 independent plants 70.

Generation of PHO2-OE lines in rice

The OsPHO2 expression cassette was assembled using Goldengate cloning 71,72. The PHO2 coding sequence (CDS) was amplified from total cDNA derived from WT rice roots grown at 250 µM Pi using Phusion™ High-fidelity DNA Polymerase (Thermo Scientific™, USA). Initial PCR was carried out to domesticate a BpiI restriction site (GAGGAC) in the PHO2 CDS (57-63 bp) from GAA to GAG before assembling into a level 0 vector. This was fused at the C-terminus to the GSyellow tag 73. Expression of the PHO2-GSyellow fusion was driven by the maize ubiquitin promoter with the Arabidopsis ACTIN2 terminator. The resulting construct was recombined into a binary vector, pEW343-R1R2 74 expressing the hygromycin phosphotransferase II (HPTII) selectable marker using the Gateway cloning system (Invitrogen, USA). The final plasmid was transferred to Agrobacterium tumefaciens strain EHA105 by electroporation. Transformation of the rice callus derived from embryo was carried out as previously described 75.

Genomic DNA extraction and genotyping

Leaf tissue was collected in 2 mL Eppendorf tubes with three autoclaved glass beads (3 mm diameter) and frozen using liquid nitrogen. Tissue was ground to powder using a tissue homogeniser (SPEX SamplePrep HG-600 Geno/Grinder® 2010, Cole-Parmer Equipment LLC., USA). Genomic (g)DNA extraction was performed by incubating the powder with 300 mL of extraction buffer (1 M KCl, 0.1 M Tris, 10 mM EDTA, adjusted to pH 7.4 with HCl in water) at 98 °C for 10 minutes. Samples were vortexed briefly, then centrifuged for 5 minutes at 13,000 rpm at room temperature, and the supernatant was

transferred to a new 1.5 mL tube containing 300 mL of 100% isopropanol and mixed by inversion. Samples were then centrifuged for 15 minutes at 13,000 rpm to form pellets. The pellet was washed twice with 500 mL of 70% (v/v) ethanol and air-dried at room temperature before being resuspended in 30 mL autoclaved ultra-pure Milli-Q water. Genotyping was performed by PCR with primers listed in Table S2.

RNA Extraction, cDNA synthesis and Reverse Transcription (RT-) quantitative (q)PCR

Approximately 60-100 mg of shoot or root tissue from each sample was collected in 2 mL safe-lock microcentrifuge tubes containing two autoclaved metal beads (3 mm diameter). The tissue was immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80 °C. Frozen samples were ground to powder (SPEX SamplePrep HG-600 Geno/Grinder® 2010, Cole-Parmer Equipment LLC., USA) and RNA was extracted using the RNeasy Plant Minikit (QIAgen, Germany), following the manufacturer’s instructions. RNA concentration was quantified by spectrophotometry (NanoDrop™ One, Thermo Scientific, USA). Absence of genomic DNA in extracted RNA was confirmed using PCR and RNA integrity was checked by gel electrophoresis on a 1% agarose gel.

For samples used in cDNA synthesis, residual genomic DNA was removed using DNase I (1 U/uL, ThermoScientific, USA). cDNA was synthesised using SuperScript IV reverse transcriptase and RNase-OUT™ (Invitrogen, USA). Success of cDNA synthesis and absence of genomic DNA contamination were confirmed using PCR. RT-qPCR was performed with primers in Table S2 using a master mix of GoTaq DNA Polymerase® (Promega Corporation, USA) and SYBR™ green (Thermo Scientific, USA) with a CFX384 Real-Time PCR Detection System (Bio-Rad laboratories Inc., USA).

RNA-sequencing and data analysis

RNA extraction and quality control were performed as described above. Genomic DNA was removed from samples for RNA-seq using TURBO DNase™ (Invitrogen, USA). The mRNA library preparation, quality control 150 bp paired-end sequencing were performed by Novogene (Cambridge, UK). Raw sequencing reads were quality-checked with MultiQC v1.26 76 and subsequently adapter-trimmed using Trimmomatic v0.39 77. High-quality reads were then quantified at the transcript level using SALMON v0.7.0 quasi-transcript mapping 78. This was performed within the Mambaforge environment on

Windows. Reads were mapped to the Oryza sativa MSU v7.0 (Rice genome annotation project, RGAP) reference transcriptome downloaded from Phytozome v.12 79. Transcript-level abundance estimates from Salmon were imported into RStudio (v4.3.1) and summarised to the gene level using the tximport package v1.28.0 80 in R/Bioconductor 3.21 81,82 before input into DESeq2 v1.40.0 83. DESeq2 was used for differential expression analysis with cut-offs for differentially expressed genes (DEGs): FDR ≤ 0.05 and an absolute fold change of ≥ 1.5. DEGs were grouped using k-means clustering. The resulting clusters were visualised as heatmaps generated using ComplexHeatmap v2.16.0 84,85 in R/Bioconductor 3.22. For downstream gene ontology (GO) enrichment MSU locus identifiers were converted to their corresponding Rice Annotation Project (RAP) 86 identifiers using the MSU-RAP cross-reference table (version dated 19 March 2025; accessed 16 February 2026) obtained from the RAP data base (-DB) website (https://rapdb.dna.affrc.go.jp/download/irgsp1.html). This conversion was performed because RAP-DB provides a broader and more updated GO term annotation set than MSU. GO enrichment was subsequently performed using the clusterProfiler v4.18.4 package 87 in R/Bioconductor 3.22 and a compilation of GO term annotations of O. sativa genes collected from the RAP-DB 86 and EnsemblPlants 88 annotations for the Os-Nipponbare-Reference-IRGSP-1.0.59 reference genome, as well as Oryzabase 89, previously described in 90.

Quantification of inorganic phosphate

Soluble inorganic phosphate was quantified from fresh plant tissue using a modified ammonium molybdate-ascorbic acid assay 91. Approximately 80-100 mg of shoot or root tissue was transferred to a 2.5 mL microcentrifuge tube containing two autoclaved metal beads (3 mm) and frozen using liquid nitrogen. Samples were homogenised to a fine powder using a tissue homogeniser (SPEX SamplePrep HG-600 Geno/Grinder® 2010, Cole-Parmer Equipment LLC., USA). Soluble inorganic phosphate was extracted by incubating the homogenised tissue in 1.5 mL sterile ultrapure Milli-Q water for 45 minutes at 85 °C with occasional inversion. Samples were briefly centrifuged, and an aliquot of the resulting supernatant (50–200 µL) was diluted to 300 µL with sterile ultrapure Milli-Q water in a new 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tube. To each aliquot, 600 µL of 3.6 M ammonium molybdate soluti

(v/v) H2SO4 (36 N)) and 100 µL 10% L-ascorbic acid (w/v with water) were added, followed by incubation at 42 °C for 1 hour to allow colour development. Absorbance was measured at 650 nm using CLARIOstar® Plus plate reader (BMG Labtech, Germany). Phosphate concentration was determined using a standard curve generated from known concentrations of K2HPO4 solution and corrected for dilution and finally normalised to tissue fresh weight.

Generating pho2 mutant in Nicotiana benthamiana

Arabidopsis thaliana PHO2 (AT2G33770) peptide sequence was used to query against the Nicotiana benthamiana genome v1.0.1 92 using the tBLASTn algorithm v2.10.1 + 93. Two genes were identified with high sequence similarity (NbPHO2a and NbPHO2b) and supported by phylogenetic analysis (Fig. S1). A single guide (sg)RNA was designed to target both PHO2 sequences and mutants were generated using Viral-induced Gene Editing, as previously described 94. Briefly, Tobacco Rattle Virus vectors were assembled expressing the NbPHO2 sgRNA augmented with a Truncated Flowering Locus T (FT) mobile RNA and used to infiltrate into 6-week-old N. benthamiana plants expressing SpCAS9. After approximately 2 months, seeds were collected, and gene editing was confirmed by the Sanger sequencing of a PCR product at the sgRNA target site (see primers in Table S2).
